# What Is Search Intent? Types and Examples

Every Google search has a reason behind it. Someone typing "how to start a blog" wants to learn, while someone typing "Bluehost login" wants to reach a specific page. That reason is called search intent, and it's one of the most important ideas in SEO.

Most searches are about finding information. A widely cited Penn State study of more than 1.5 million search queries found that about **80%** of searches were informational, about **10%** navigational and about **10%** transactional, according to [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/study-80-percent-of-searches-are-informational-20-are-navigational-or-transactional-13745). The study is older, but it still shows how much of search is driven by people wanting to learn.

Intent also shapes what Google shows. Semrush analyzed more than **600,000 keywords** and found that the share of commercial-intent results showing an AI Overview grew **71%** between November 2025 and April 2026, according to its [AI Overviews study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-commercial-search-study/), published July 2, 2026.

In this guide, I'll explain what search intent is, the four main types with examples, how to identify the intent behind any keyword, how to match your content to it and the common mistakes to avoid.

## What Is Search Intent?

**Search intent, also called user intent or keyword intent, is the main goal a person has when they type a query into a search engine.**

It answers one simple question: **What does this searcher want?**

They might want to:

- **Learn something**
- **Find a specific website**
- **Compare products or services**
- **Buy something or take an action**

Google aims to show results that best satisfy that goal. If your content doesn't match the intent, it's unlikely to rank, no matter how well written it is.

## Why Search Intent Matters for SEO

**Search intent matters because Google ranks content that best satisfies what searchers want.**

- **Better rankings:** Content that matches intent is more likely to rank
- **More relevant traffic:** You attract people who want what you offer
- **Higher engagement:** Visitors find what they expected
- **Better conversions:** Commercial and transactional pages reach people ready to act
- **Smarter content planning:** You choose the right content type for each keyword

Ahrefs' study of about 14 billion pages found that **96.55%** of pages get no Google traffic, and mismatched search intent was one of the main reasons.

**The four main types of search intent are informational, navigational, commercial and transactional.**

## 1. Informational Intent

**Informational intent means the searcher wants to learn something, find an answer or understand a topic.**

### Examples

- **how to start a blog**
- **what is SEO**
- **why is my website slow**
- **best time to post on Instagram**
- **Linux history**

### Common Words

**How, what, why, when, who, guide, tutorial, tips, examples, ideas**

### Best Content Types

- **Blog posts and guides**
- **Tutorials and how-tos**
- **Definitions and explainers**
- **Videos and infographics**

### What Google Often Shows

- **Featured snippets**
- **"People also ask" boxes**
- **Videos**
- **AI Overviews**

## 2. Navigational Intent

**Navigational intent means the searcher wants to reach a specific website, brand or page.**

### Examples

- **Semrush login**
- **YouTube**
- **Gmail sign in**
- **WordPress.org plugins**
- **Canva templates**

### Common Words

**Brand names, product names, login, sign in, contact, official website**

### Best Content Types

- **Homepages**
- **Login pages**
- **Brand and product pages**
- **Contact and support pages**

**Note:** It's usually hard to rank for another brand's navigational keywords, because searchers want that brand's official site.

## 3. Commercial Intent

**Commercial intent means the searcher is researching products, services or brands before making a decision.**

### Examples

- **best SEO tools for bloggers**
- **Semrush vs Ahrefs**
- **Grammarly review**
- **best cheap web hosting**
- **Canva Pro worth it**

### Common Words

**Best, top, review, vs, comparison, alternatives, worth it, pros and cons**

### Best Content Types

- **Product reviews**
- **Comparison posts**
- **"Best of" lists**
- **Buying guides**
- **Alternatives articles**

### Why It Matters

Commercial-intent keywords are valuable for affiliate bloggers, because searchers are close to buying. They're also increasingly showing AI Overviews, according to Semrush's 2026 study.

## 4. Transactional Intent

**Transactional intent means the searcher is ready to buy, sign up or take a specific action.**

### Examples

- **buy Canva Pro**
- **Semrush free trial**
- **Hostinger coupon code**
- **download VLC**
- **sign up for Kit**

### Common Words

**Buy, price, discount, coupon, deal, free trial, sign up, download, order**

### Best Content Types

- **Product and pricing pages**
- **Sign-up and checkout pages**
- **Coupon and deal pages**
- **Free trial pages**

## Search Intent Types at a Glance

| Intent | Searcher wants to | Example | Best content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informational | Learn something | how to start a blog | Guides, tutorials |
| Navigational | Reach a specific site | Semrush login | Homepage, login page |
| Commercial | Compare options | best SEO tools | Reviews, comparisons, lists |
| Transactional | Take action or buy | Semrush free trial | Pricing, deal and sign-up pages |

## Mixed Intent Keywords

**Some keywords have more than one intent, so Google shows a mix of result types.**

For example, "Semrush" could mean:

- **Navigational:** Go to the Semrush website
- **Informational:** Learn what Semrush is
- **Commercial:** Read Semrush reviews

When Google shows mixed results, consider which intent best fits your content, or cover several needs in one page.

## Google's View of User Intent

**Google's own Search Quality Rater Guidelines describe user intent in simple categories.**

Google's [Search Quality Rater Guidelines](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/hsw-sqrg.pdf) describe intents like:

- **Know:** The user wants information
- **Do:** The user wants to accomplish a goal or activity
- **Website:** The user wants a specific website or page
- **Visit-in-person:** The user wants to visit a local place

These map closely to the four SEO intent types, with an added focus on local searches.

## How to Identify Search Intent

**The best way to identify search intent is to look at what Google already ranks for that keyword.**

### 1. Analyze the Search Results

**Search the keyword in Google and look at the top results.**

Ask:

- **What type of pages rank?** Blog posts, product pages, videos or tools?
- **What format do they use?** How-to guides, lists, reviews or comparisons?
- **What angle do they take?** Beginner, cheap, fast, best for a specific group?

### 2. Look at Keyword Modifiers

**Words in the keyword often reveal intent.**

- **"How," "what," "guide"** usually mean informational
- **"Best," "review," "vs"** usually mean commercial
- **"Buy," "coupon," "free trial"** usually mean transactional
- **Brand names alone** often mean navigational

### 3. Check SERP Features

**Special results hint at intent.**

- **Featured snippets and "People also ask":** Informational
- **Shopping results and ads:** Commercial or transactional
- **Sitelinks:** Navigational
- **Local map pack:** Local intent

### 4. Use an SEO Tool

**Tools like Semrush label keyword intent automatically.**

In Semrush's Keyword Magic Tool and Keyword Overview, each keyword is tagged with an intent label, so you can filter keywords by intent while planning content.

## How to Match Content to Search Intent

**Match your content's type, format and angle to what searchers expect.**

### Content Type

**Choose the right kind of page.**

- **Informational:** Blog post or guide
- **Commercial:** Review, comparison or list
- **Transactional:** Product, pricing or deal page

### Content Format

**Use the format top results use.**

- **How-to guides** for step-by-step questions
- **Listicles** for "best" keywords
- **Comparison tables** for "vs" keywords

### Content Angle

**Match the unique angle searchers want.**

Examples include "for beginners," "free," "in 2026," "step by step" and "for small businesses."

### Example: Matching Intent

**Keyword:** best SEO tools for bloggers

- **Intent:** Commercial
- **Content type:** Blog post
- **Format:** List with comparisons
- **Angle:** Bloggers, free and paid options

Matching intent also works alongside other ranking factors. This guide to the [top SEO ranking factors](https://sites.google.com/view/top-3-seo-ranking-factors) explains how they fit together.

## How Search Intent Fits the Buyer Journey

**Different intent types match different stages of the buyer journey.**

- **Awareness stage:** Informational keywords, like "what is keyword research"
- **Consideration stage:** Commercial keywords, like "best keyword research tools"
- **Decision stage:** Transactional keywords, like "Semrush free trial"

A strong content strategy covers all three stages, so you can guide readers from learning to buying.

## Search Intent and AI Search

**AI Overviews and AI search tools also try to answer intent directly.**

- **Informational searches** often get AI-generated summaries
- **Commercial searches** increasingly show AI Overviews, according to Semrush's 2026 study
- **Clear, well-structured content** with direct answers is easier for AI tools to understand and cite

To stay visible, write clear answers near the top of your content, use descriptive headings and cover related questions.

## Common Search Intent Mistakes

**The biggest mistakes are mismatching content type, ignoring the search results and targeting keywords with the wrong intent.**

- **Writing a blog post** for a keyword that needs a product page
- **Creating a sales page** for an informational keyword
- **Ignoring what already ranks**
- **Targeting navigational keywords** for other brands
- **Missing the angle**, like "for beginners" or "free"
- **Not updating content** when intent changes over time

## How to Use Search Intent in Your Keyword Research

2. **Label each keyword's intent**
3. **Check the top results** to confirm it
4. **Group keywords by intent and topic**
5. **Plan the right content type** for each group
6. **Track rankings and engagement**, then refine

## Final Thoughts

Search intent is the reason behind a search, and matching it is essential for ranking on Google. The four main types are informational, navigational, commercial and transactional, and each needs a different type of content.

Most searches are informational, with a widely cited Penn State study putting the figure at about 80%. But commercial and transactional keywords are often more valuable for bloggers and businesses.
